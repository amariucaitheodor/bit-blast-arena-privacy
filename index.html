1. Introduction & Scope

Bit Blast: Arena is a fast-paced pixel-art multiplayer shooter. This policy explains exactly what information leaves your device when you play, why it leaves, who receives it, and how to stop it.

It applies to every platform and version of the game, including builds distributed through Google Play. It covers the game itself and the matchmaking relay we operate. It does not cover Google Play, your device manufacturer, or your network provider, each of which has its own policy.

By downloading or playing Bit Blast: Arena you agree to the practices described here. If you do not agree, please do not use the game.

2. Gameplay Analytics

The game sends anonymous gameplay statistics to PostHog, a product-analytics service, so we can see which weapons are overpowered, which devices drop frames, and where players get stuck. This is the only routine data collection in the game.

HOW TO TURN IT OFF Open the game, tap SETTINGS on the main menu, and tap the ANALYTICS button until it reads ANALYTICS: OFF. Collection stops immediately, any events still waiting on your device are discarded, and the choice is remembered on that device from then on. The game plays exactly the same either way.

What is in an analytics event

  • A random identifier. The first time you launch the game it generates 16 random bytes and saves them on your device. That value is attached to your events so we can tell one player’s rounds apart from another’s. It is not derived from anything about you or your hardware, and it is not your device’s advertising ID. It is not connected to any account, name, email, or contact detail, and we hold nothing that would let us connect it to a real person. Uninstalling the game deletes it.
  • Round summaries. Game mode, transport (offline, local Wi-Fi, or internet), how long the round lasted, whether you won or lost, remaining health, and counts of in-game events such as overloads and elemental reactions.
  • Weapon and ability usage. Which weapons and abilities you used, shots fired, hits landed, damage dealt, and critical-hit types.
  • In-game economy. Which weapons, abilities, and items you bought with in-game coins, and your coin balance afterwards. All currency in Bit Blast: Arena is in-game only — the game contains no real-money purchases and never handles payment information.
  • Menu navigation. Which screen a button was pressed on and the button’s fixed label (for example JUMP IN). Text you type yourself, such as your display name or a room code, is never included.
  • Performance. Median and 5th-percentile frame rate for a round, and how many seconds were sampled.
  • Device context. Platform (Android or iOS), device model, screen resolution, language, and the game’s version number. This is hardware-class information used to reproduce layout and performance bugs; it is not detailed enough to identify a device.
  • Approximate location — country only. PostHog uses the IP address your events arrive from to work out which country they came from, and then discards the address instead of storing it. We keep the country and continent and deliberately nothing finer: no city, no region, no postal code, and no coordinates. We use it only to understand where our players are. We never receive or request precise or GPS location, and the game holds no location permission.

How it is sent and stored

  • Events are batched on your device and sent over HTTPS roughly every 30 seconds.
  • They go to PostHog’s European Union infrastructure (eu.i.posthog.com), where they are stored.
  • PostHog processes this data on our behalf and under our instructions. They do not use it for their own purposes, and we do not sell or share it with anyone else. See PostHog’s privacy policy.
  • Analytics events are retained for no more than 12 months, after which they are deleted.
  • Builds we run ourselves during development are tagged as internal and excluded from our analysis.

3. Multiplayer & Connection Data

Your display name

Before a match you may enter a display name. If you do not, the game invents one such as Player417. This name is saved on your device only so you do not have to retype it, and it is sent to the other players in your match so they can see who they are fighting. We do not store it on any server, it is never attached to your analytics data, and it is not linked to any account or real identity. Please do not use your real name.

Your IP address

Connecting to another player necessarily reveals a network address, as it does in any online game:

  • Local Wi-Fi play. Devices connect directly to each other on your own network and nothing reaches us at all. Because this traffic stays on your local network, it is not encrypted.
  • Internet play. Your device opens an encrypted connection (WSS) to our relay server, which forwards game packets between the players in a room. The relay holds your connection’s network address in memory for the length of the session purely to route packets. It writes nothing to disk, keeps no database, does not read the contents of the packets it forwards, and discards the room entirely once the last player leaves. The relay runs on Deno Deploy.
  • Analytics. Separately from matchmaking, your analytics events reach PostHog from an IP address. It is used once, at the moment the event arrives, to determine your country, and is then discarded rather than stored — it is not kept against your events or your random identifier. Turning analytics off stops this.

7. Children’s Privacy

Bit Blast: Arena is suitable for all ages and its Google Play target audience includes children. We have built it accordingly: there is no account creation, no chat, no advertising, no in-app purchasing, and no request for a real name, age, email, or contact details at any point.

The game does generate and transmit a persistent random identifier with its analytics events, and we want to be explicit about that. Under the U.S. Children’s Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) a persistent identifier is personal information, but collecting one is permitted without parental consent when it is used solely for support for the internal operations of the service. That is the only basis we rely on. Specifically:

  • The identifier is random and generated on-device. It is not the Android advertising ID, and the game never reads the advertising ID.
  • It is used only to group a single device’s rounds together so we can measure game balance, performance, and where players get stuck.
  • It is never used for advertising, behavioural profiling, personalisation, cross-app or cross-site tracking, audience building, or contacting a player.
  • The only location information is the country an event arrived from. COPPA treats geolocation as personal information when it is sufficient to identify a street, city, or town; a country is not, and we deliberately keep nothing finer — no city, no region, no postal code, no coordinates.
  • Nothing collected is disclosed to any third party for their own purposes.
  • Collection can be switched off entirely in Settings, by a child or a parent, with no effect on gameplay.

We apply the same standard under the UK Age Appropriate Design Code and the GDPR’s protections for children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from a child beyond what this policy describes.

Parents and guardians: if you would prefer that nothing at all is collected, turn analytics off in Settings — that is the complete fix and it requires no request to us. If you have questions or want data deleted, contact us using section 10 and we will respond promptly.

8. Your Choices & Rights

  • Stop collection. Settings → ANALYTICS: OFF. Immediate, remembered on that device, and reversible at any time.
  • Delete everything locally. Uninstalling the game removes the random identifier, your display name, and all other saved data from your device.
  • Play with nothing leaving your device. Solo practice and local Wi-Fi matches with analytics off involve no communication with us at all.
  • Access, correction, deletion, objection, and portability. If you are in the EU/EEA, the UK, or a jurisdiction with comparable rights, you may exercise them by contacting us. We do not need to verify an identity because we hold none, and we will not ask you for one.

Deleting your data

Requesting deletion of analytics data. Email lakesidetheory@gmail.com with the subject “Data Deletion Request”. Because we hold no account and no contact details, we cannot identify your specific records from an email alone. In practice the reliable way to erase your data is to turn analytics off and uninstall the game, which deletes the identifier your events were grouped under and leaves the remaining records unattributable. If you write to us we will confirm this and action any request we are able to within 30 days.

Legal basis (GDPR). We rely on legitimate interests for analytics — improving a game we give away — balanced by making collection easy to refuse and by never collecting identifying information. Multiplayer connection data is processed to perform the service you asked for.

International transfers. Analytics data is stored on PostHog’s EU infrastructure. Our relay runs on Deno Deploy’s global edge network, which may route your session through a region near you; it stores nothing anywhere.

9. Third-Party Services

Three third parties are involved, and only three:

  • PostHog (EU Cloud) — product analytics, as described in section 2. Acts as our data processor. Privacy policy.
  • Deno Deploy — hosts our matchmaking relay, which stores nothing. Privacy policy.

There are no advertising networks, no attribution or install-tracking SDKs, no social-media SDKs, and no crash-reporting SDKs in the game.
